Rica Vista Tenant Help

A proof of concept for resident requests and staff notification
1825 Poggi Street, Alameda, CA

One-page proposal
November 2026

The opportunity

Residents do not always know who to contact when something needs attention — a leak, a lockout, a gate that will not close, or a concern after the office has closed. Messages can land in different inboxes or phone threads, and it is not always clear whether a request was received, who is handling it, or when it was resolved. After-hours issues are especially easy to miss if the right on-call person is not in the thread.

How it works

A resident opens a simple web portal or an installable phone app, chooses a unit and a category, and submits a short description. The system lists the staff who should be notified, based on rules the manager can edit at any time — including more than one person, and a follow-up if no one has acknowledged the request after a set number of minutes. Each request keeps a status timeline the tenant can see: submitted, acknowledged, in progress, and done.

This demonstration pages staff by opening a text message on the resident’s (or staff member’s) own phone, already addressed and written, ready to send. A call button is available as well, especially for emergencies, and emergency requests remind people to call 911 first if anyone is in danger. A production version would send those texts and calls automatically from a dedicated building number (for example, through Twilio), without using anyone’s personal messaging plan, and would escalate on its own if a request stays unacknowledged.

Benefits

For residents

  • One obvious place to ask for help, on the web or as a phone app
  • No guessing which number to use, including after hours
  • A record that the request was received and when it was resolved

For on-site staff

  • Requests arrive in a consistent format, with unit, category, and urgency
  • Routing and phone numbers are easy to change as roles or schedules change
  • A shared inbox and page log, instead of scattered texts

For ownership

  • A documented history of requests and response times
  • Faster, more reliable after-hours coverage with clear escalation
  • A modest operating cost for a single building, with a short pilot path

What would be needed to go live

  • Confirm staff names, roles, phone numbers, coverage hours, and a routing rule for each request type.
  • A small hosted backend (such as Supabase or Firebase) so requests stay in sync across devices, plus a building telephone number for automatic texts and calls.
  • US A2P 10DLC registration, the standard carrier process for business texting, so messages come from an identified building or management sender.
  • A brief resident notice explaining how to use the portal or app, and when to call 911 instead.
  • Optional later step: a store-listed iOS or Android app (Capacitor or Expo). Apple’s developer program is $99 per year; Google Play registration is $25, one time. The current demo already works as an installable web app.

Estimated monthly cost for one building

Based on Twilio’s published US rates as of November 2026: local number $1.15/month; outbound SMS $0.0083 per segment plus carrier fees of about $0.0025–$0.005; outbound voice $0.014/minute. A2P 10DLC low-volume registration is about $20 one time and $1.50/month. Backend hosting can often stay on a free tier for one property. Typical ongoing cost: about $5–$15 per month at modest volume. Vendor prices can change.

A suggested 30-day pilot

Run Tenant Help alongside current practice for 30 days, starting with a short list of categories (water, lockout, access, after-hours emergency, general maintenance). Review the page log and response times at the midpoint and at month’s end. If the pilot is useful, move from phone-composed texts to automatic sending from a building number — without changing what residents see.
